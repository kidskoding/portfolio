It took 73 hours.

It took 128 attempts.

And the final blow went to the player who died three times in his first two minutes.

On October 5, Jynxzi, StableRonaldo (Ron) and LosPollos (Los) went live with a simple promise: they would play hardcore Minecraft around the clock and **not end the stream until they beat the game**. Just over three days later, Los killed the Ender Dragon by himself.

On the surface, it was three streamers yelling at each other in a block game for three days straight. **But the way they set up that run is one of the best lessons in teamwork I've seen this year, and it holds up in any office.**

## The rule that changed everything

Before the marathon started, Jynxzi announced the rules. There would be **no enchantments and no totems** (the item that saves you from one death), which made an already brutal mode even harder.

Then came the rule that made the whole thing interesting: **Los had to deal the main damage to the Ender Dragon.**

<TweetEmbed url="https://x.com/scubaryan_/status/2107232155319545965" />

That's a strange rule if all you care about is winning, especially once the run started with Los dying three times in under two minutes. Los himself wasn't sold on the plan when Jynxzi called to walk him through it, joking that a 10-day marathon sounded like a way to run max ads.

A team that only cared about the fastest clear would have let its strongest player take the dragon. Instead, **Jynxzi set the goal up so the win would belong to the person most likely to struggle with it.** From that moment on, the job of the other two was no longer "beat the game" but rather "get Los to the point where he can beat it."

> Jynxzi and Ron weren't playing to win anymore. They were playing to get Los to the win.

---

## Jynxzi had already lost this once

What makes that choice land is that Jynxzi had been here before.

### The 11 hours he lost in May

In May, he did a hardcore marathon with just Ron. More than 11 hours into one attempt, he swung at an End Crystal at point-blank range during the dragon fight, with Ron yelling at him not to. **The explosion killed him and the run, and he teared up on stream.** Later in the same marathon, 32 hours in, Ron died to an Enderman in The End. They finally beat it on attempt 78, after 73 hours and 77 deaths.

### Why he made it harder anyway

So when Jynxzi planned the October run, he knew exactly how painful hardcore is. **He chose to make it harder anyway, in a way that took the spotlight off himself.**

That's what makes him inspiring to me. He built a career on Rainbow Six Siege, on Xbox, on rage clips that made people laugh at him, and went on to win Gamer of the Year at the 2024 Streamer Awards. **Somebody with that much attention could easily make every event about himself, and instead he built this one around a friend.**

## What this looks like at work

None of this stays in Minecraft. Every lesson from the run maps onto a real team, and these are the four I keep coming back to.

### 1. Give away the final hit

In a lot of workplaces, the senior person takes the hard ticket, gives the big demo and fixes the outage at 2 AM. That gets things done, but it also means the rest of the team never gets the reps. The Ender Dragon rule flips it: **the most valuable thing a strong player can do is clear the path so someone newer gets the win.**

At work, that looks like giving the launch demo to the person who built the feature, letting a junior engineer drive the incident call while you sit next to them, or handing off the interesting problem with enough context that they can actually finish it.

As an intern, I've noticed the engineers I learn the most from are the ones who do this. They could take the hard task in half the time, but they'd rather stay close and let me take it. **It costs something in the short term, but the payoff is a team where more than one person can kill the dragon.**

### 2. Every reset belongs to the whole team

The attempt counter was shared, so the stream tracked 128 attempts for the group rather than a score for each player. With 127 deaths across 128 attempts, **every death, no matter whose, sent all three of them back to a fresh world with nothing.**

That's how real projects work too. When a deploy breaks production, it doesn't matter much whose commit it was, because everyone is rolling back together. **Teams that handle this well ask what made the mistake easy to make, not who made it**, which is the whole idea behind a blameless postmortem.

### 3. Check the run before you blame the new person

The funniest moment of the marathon came after it ended. A viewer put together a breakdown of the deaths that showed **Jynxzi, not Los, was the weakest link**, and Los couldn't believe it.

Everyone had assumed Los was the problem, and the data said otherwise. **The person a team assumes is holding it back often isn't**, and you only find that out if you look at the run instead of the reputation.

### 4. Let people see you lose

The part of the May run I keep thinking about isn't the mistake, but rather what came after it. Jynxzi kept playing, finished the run with Ron, and months later signed up to do it all again with a rule that made it harder.

Everybody has a version of the End Crystal. It might be a branch you lost, a project that got cancelled, or an interview where you froze on a problem you knew. **Getting upset about it is normal, and Jynxzi showed that it's fine to let people see it.** What matters is what you do with the next attempt, and he used his to set up a friend.

> Everybody has a version of the End Crystal. What matters is what you do with the next attempt.

## The kind of teammate I want to be

I don't think anyone watching expected a hardcore Minecraft stream to say something about teamwork. Most of it was chaos, deaths and three friends roasting each other.

**But the structure underneath was a real lesson: decide what winning means for the team, then build it around the person who needs the win the most.** That's the kind of teammate I want to be, and the kind of team I want to work on.

It took 73 hours and 128 attempts.

Ron and Jynxzi could have taken the dragon themselves.

They let Los hit it instead.
